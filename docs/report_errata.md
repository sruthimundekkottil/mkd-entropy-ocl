# Corrections to the M.Sc. report "Variance-Regularized Online Continual Learning" (2 June 2026)

The M.Sc. report itself is not published in this repository. This file lists the corrections to it: every statement in the report that the code or the saved result files do not support, with the corrected wording. Section, table and figure numbers refer to the report.
All numbers below come from tables generated by `scripts/make_report_tables.py` from the files listed in `results/manifest.csv`. Local-run numbers (the headline results) are in `docs/results_tables.md`. Kaggle-run numbers are marked *(archive)* and are in `results/archive_kaggle/archive_tables.md`: those runs are archived because their code version cannot be confirmed. Where each folder came from is recorded in `results/PROVENANCE.md`.

## 1. Metric names (Sections 4.1, Tables 4.1 and 4.2, Abstract, Chapter 5)

**Report:** "Average Accuracy is the mean per-task accuracy across all 5 tasks measured after training on the final task." "Forgetting is the mean decrease … to after all 5 tasks are complete."

**Correction:** the reported numbers are **average incremental accuracy**, the mean over the 5 task checkpoints of the average accuracy on the tasks seen so far, and **average incremental forgetting**, the same averaging applied to forgetting. See `np.nanmean(accs)` in the old `run_local.py` (now `scripts/run_vrocl.py`, `aia`/`avg_fgt`). Accuracy and forgetting measured once after the last task are reported alongside them as **final accuracy** and **final forgetting**.

Table 4.1 with both metric pairs (local runs, mean ± std over seeds 0–2):

| Method | μ | Avg. incremental acc. | Final acc. | Avg. incremental forgetting | Final forgetting |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ER | – | 70.38 ± 2.72 | 52.73 ± 5.56 | 24.79 ± 3.50 | 33.02 ± 3.67 |
| EWC | – | 70.69 ± 2.26 | 56.30 ± 2.23 | 22.12 ± 1.52 | 28.05 ± 1.76 |
| VR-OCL Fixed | 0.0005 | 70.71 ± 2.57 | 56.98 ± 2.45 | 20.22 ± 4.75 | 26.29 ± 4.42 |
| VR-OCL Decay | 0.0005 | 69.94 ± 1.80 | 56.03 ± 3.42 | 18.89 ± 6.18 | 25.47 ± 3.17 |
| VR-OCL Adaptive | μmax 0.02, τ 0.5 | 65.48 ± 0.53 | 49.17 ± 1.77 | 19.71 ± 8.02 | 27.46 ± 4.05 |

The first and third numeric columns are exactly the report's Table 4.1.

## 2. Learning rate (Section 4.1)
**Report:** "Adam with learning rate 0.001." **Correction:** 0.0005. This is the parser default (`config/parser.py:23`) and the value in every saved `params_used.json`.

## 3. Table 4.2 mixes two environments, and one row has no files
- The μ = 0.01 and μ = 0.005 rows are **Kaggle** runs. The μ = 0.0005 row and the ER baseline quoted in the caption (70.38 / 24.79) are **local** runs. The two environments give different ER results for the same seeds and settings: Kaggle ER is 69.21 ± 2.47 / 17.15 ± 0.99 *(archive)*. So the caption's comparison is not like-for-like.
- Compared with the **Kaggle** ER, every Kaggle VR row has lower mean average-incremental accuracy and higher mean average-incremental forgetting. Per seed (`results/archive_kaggle/archive_tables.md`, "Summary of the per-seed differences"), these rows have lower average incremental forgetting than ER in at most 1 of 3 seeds. Final forgetting is lower in 1–2 of 3 seeds.
- The **μ = 0.001 row** (68.83 / 18.15; 69.27 / 16.80) exists only as printed output in the author's private Kaggle notebook (not in this repository). Its per-run files were overwritten, so it is unverified (`results/PROVENANCE.md`). A local run at μ = 0.001 gives 68.85 ± 1.71 / 20.57 ± 2.26 (Fixed) and 67.24 ± 2.74 / 20.31 ± 4.87 (Decay).
- **Correction:** keep only rows with files, and never compare across environments:
  - headline (local): μ ∈ {0.001, 0.0005}, compared with the local ER and EWC (the "μ ablation" section of `docs/results_tables.md`);
  - Kaggle μ ∈ {0.01, 0.005} can only be reported as archived runs with an unconfirmed code version, compared with the Kaggle ER (`results/archive_kaggle/archive_tables.md`);
  - the unverified Kaggle μ = 0.001 row is dropped.

## 4. Claims that depend on Table 4.2 (Sections 4.2.2, 4.2.3, 4.3, 5.2, 5.3)
- "Accuracy rises monotonically as μ decreases." Within each environment, only two μ values have files. In both environments, the smaller μ gives the higher mean average-incremental accuracy for both variants. A four-point monotonic trend cannot be claimed because the four points come from two environments, and one of them is unverified.
- "Forgetting is minimised at μ = 0.001 (18.15%)" and "VR-OCL Decay reaches its minimum forgetting at μ = 0.001 (16.80%)", including the whole "Decay Schedule Finding" paragraph and the "Task-Difficulty-Aware Schedule" motivation in 5.3. These rest on the unverified row. **Remove them**, or rerun μ = 0.001 alongside its own ER baseline.
- "The ablation over μ demonstrates a clear and monotonic stability-plasticity tradeoff." Replace with: "In the local runs, the smaller of the two μ values tested (0.0005 vs 0.001) gave higher mean average incremental accuracy; the forgetting differences are within one standard deviation."

## 5. Size of the forgetting reduction (Abstract, 4.2.1, 4.3, 5.2)
"Reduces forgetting by 18% relative to ER and 9% relative to EWC": the arithmetic on the local means is correct (24.79 → 20.22 and 22.12 → 20.22). Please add:
- the spread across seeds is large (± 4.75 for VR-OCL Fixed);
- per seed, VR-OCL Fixed μ = 0.0005 has lower average incremental forgetting than ER in 2 of 3 seeds, and lower final forgetting in 3 of 3;
- this holds for the local runs only. No Kaggle VR configuration has lower mean average-incremental forgetting than the Kaggle ER *(archive)*.

With 3 seeds, this is not evidence of a significant difference.

## 6. "VR-OCL Decay achieves the lowest forgetting (18.89%)" (Abstract, 4.2.1, 5.2)
Add "among the methods in Table 4.1 (local runs)". Its std is ± 6.18, which overlaps with every other method.

## 7. Task boundaries (Abstract, 4.2.1, 4.3, 5.2)
**Report:** "without any Fisher computation or task boundary detection." **Correction:** VR-OCL does not *detect* task boundaries, but it *uses* the benchmark's task boundaries to refresh its snapshot (`vr_ocl.py`, `train()`). Section 3.3 already says this, so the other sections should match it.

## 8. Decay schedule (Section 3.2, "VR-OCL Decay")
**Report:** "directly implements the paper's growing schedule μ_n = μ·β^{-n}." **Correction:** the code uses μ_t = μ·(1 − β^{task_step}). That is a different, saturating schedule bounded by μ, and with β = 0.99 it reaches 99% of μ after about 460 of the roughly 1000 steps in a task. It is *inspired by* the growing schedule of Theorem 4.2 but is not the same. The cap `vr_mu_cap` = 0.05 never takes effect for μ ≤ 0.01. Despite the name, μ grows within each task.

## 9. Adaptive μ range and the explanation of its accuracy loss (Sections 3.2, 4.2.3)
**Report:** "The sigmoid maps the smoothed gap to a value between 0 and μmax", and "in later tasks the gap ema trends toward near-zero values … causes μ to be set at moderate values". **Correction:** the accuracy-based gap is never negative, so μ = μmax·σ(gap/τ) is always between μmax/2 and μmax. For the reported run, the saved `gap_history.csv` files show μ between 0.010 and 0.014. That is 20–28 times the best fixed μ (0.0005), and it is a likely cause of the lower accuracy.

## 10. Adaptive hyperparameter search (Section 3.2)
**Report:** "μmax and τ were selected through a small hyperparameter search over {0.02, 0.05} × {0.3, 0.5}." **Correction:** result files exist for three of the four combinations:
- (0.02, 0.5): local, 65.48
- (0.05, 0.3): local, 62.14
- (0.05, 0.5): Kaggle, 63.44 *(archive)*

The (0.05, 0.5) run used the older loss-gap version of the adaptive code, so it is not directly comparable. No files exist for (0.02, 0.3).

## 11. Figures 4.1 and 4.2
Fig. 4.1 plotted the Adaptive variant at 62.14 / 19.02, which is the (0.05, 0.3) run, not the 65.48 / 19.71 in Table 4.1. Fig. 4.2 included the unverified μ = 0.001 row and joined points from two environments. Both have been regenerated from the result files by `generate_figures.py`, using the local runs only: `report_figures/fig3_scatter.*` and `report_figures/fig4_ablation.*`.

## 12. Unsupported statements
- 5.2: "Direct transfer of the paper's growing decay schedule to OCL requires modification because growing μ in later tasks inhibits plasticity." No run of the non-reset schedule exists. Remove it, or mark it as a hypothesis.
- 2.2 and 4.2.3: "Fisher estimates computed from 500 buffer samples are often too noisy to be reliable." This was not measured here. Mark it as a motivation, not a finding. Also note that `ewc.py` estimates the Fisher from squared mini-batch-mean gradients (batch 32), which is a coarser estimate than per-sample squared gradients.
